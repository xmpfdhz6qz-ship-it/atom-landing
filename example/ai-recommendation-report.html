How AI recommends in activewear,

actually stands.

An example report: what ChatGPT recommends when a shopper asks for activewear, where this brand lands, who wins instead, the language gap that explains why, and how much of any single answer is noise.

How this was measured. We asked ChatGPT 20 high intent activewear shopping questions, each run 20 times to capture variance, for 400 recommendation observations. For every answer we recorded which brands were recommended and in what position, and the reason the model gave. The client brand is anonymized; competitor names are shown as the model recommended them. Measured on ChatGPT; other AI models are being added.
Executive summary · for the CEO

When shoppers ask AI what activewear to buy, this brand is named in 15 percent of conversations. It is completely absent from the other 85 percent.

The brands AI recommends ahead of it, Nike, Under Armour, and Adidas, do not win on product quality. They win because AI understands them as performance brands. AI understands this brand as a fashion brand, so it quietly routes every performance question elsewhere.

This is not a visibility problem that more ads or SEO will fix. It is a recommendation problem, and the fix starts with language, not the website. Three concrete moves are set out later in this report.

Part 4 · What we tested and found nothing

Most of what agencies measure does not move this number.

Most AI visibility tools score on site technical quality: schema markup, page speed, structured data. We tested whether any of that predicts whether AI recommends a brand.

Fixing a website will not fix this. The AI does not grade page speed or schema markup, it already formed an opinion about a brand from what it learned long before today. Which AI model answers, and whether it is browsing the live web, moves the outcome far more than anything a technical audit can touch.

We checked 599 audited stores against a catalog of 60,325 and found a correlation of 0.7 percent, close to noise. Swapping the underlying AI model, same prompts, same day, changes 68.9 percent of recommendations, a 95 percent confidence interval of 66 to 71 percent, permutation test p under 0.001. Running the same query three days apart shows no measurable drift, p equals 0.73, statistically the same as measuring nothing twice. And once a brand is a repeat winner, its position barely moves, 61.4 percent rank stability from June to July, the number worth tracking over time, not a single screenshot.

Part 5 · Methodology · why one query is a coin flip

Ask the same question twice and the answer changes, so one screenshot proves nothing.

Run the identical question against the identical model on the identical day, and the two answers still disagree. That is the baseline every claim in this report has to be read against.

The floor falls for the first three runs, then goes flat at 46.1 percent and does not move again. It is a property of the model itself, a stable core of brands it names almost every time, and a long tail it shuffles on every call. Every number in this report is built from repeated runs, not one lucky or unlucky query.

Turning web search on drops the noise floor from 47 to 27 percent. In activewear specifically, turning search on changes 60.8 percent of what gets recommended, against a permutation null median of 24.2 percent, p equals 0.0001, in line with the 76.9 percent swing measured across all ten categories together. This early read covers 5 of the 20 buyer questions in the category, the rest is the next data pull already scoped as part of onboarding.

Part 6 · Where to start · the three moves the data actually supports

Fix the words before you fix the website.

This is not a guarantee that a number moves by a fixed amount. It is the three places this dataset points to first, in priority order.

01

Close the language gap

Rewrite the product and category pages the brand controls to use the vocabulary AI already rewards in this category: moisture wicking, durability, cushioning, stability, performance fabrics, instead of style led language alone.

02

Answer the missing questions directly

Publish content and product pages that speak plainly to the buyer questions the brand is currently invisible on. AI tends to recommend what it can clearly match to the question being asked.

03

Stop judging visibility by one chat

Results shift by up to 68.9 percent when the model changes and roughly 60.8 percent when search is toggled. Track recommendations across models and search conditions, on a schedule.

We cannot promise that doing these three things moves the brand from 15 to 30 percent, nobody honestly can yet. What we can say is these are the only three places in this dataset where the brand's own actions, rather than the model's mood that day, plausibly make a difference.

The old question was whether people could find the brand.
The new question is whether AI recommends it, and as what.

This brand does not have a quality problem. It has a framing problem, sitting on top of a measurement problem most reports never disclose. We do not claim a button that moves a brand up on command. We make the invisible decision visible, and we show how much of it is signal before anyone acts on it.

The new job of marketing.

The direction is already set out above: close the language gap, answer the missing performance questions, and track recommendations across models and search conditions instead of trusting one afternoon's chat. Getting AI to describe a brand the way the brand describes itself is not a side project anymore. It is becoming the main job of marketing, the same way ranking on Google search became the main job for a previous generation of marketers.
