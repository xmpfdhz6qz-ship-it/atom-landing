Founder's Reality Check · Entry 005

The Day I Almost Quit.

Daniel Pokorny · Solo founder, Atom Foundry

Nobody tells you this part.

People talk about building products. Launching startups. Getting customers.

Nobody talks about what happens when you have done everything you know how to do, and nothing happens.

After months of building Atom Foundry, everything was finally online.

The platform worked. The research was published. The website was live. The outreach had started.

I sent more than 300 cold emails.

I waited.

Nothing.

Not one positive reply.

Silence.

That is when the real conversation started.

Not with customers.

With myself.

Maybe this doesn't matter.

Maybe I'm trying to build something nobody wants.

Maybe the companies selling AI visibility are right, and I'm wrong.

Can I actually do this?

Can my family handle this if I don't earn anything for much longer?

Our savings are slowly disappearing.

Should I just find a regular job again?

Monday to Friday. A salary every month. No uncertainty. No pressure.

Just peace.

Then the next morning, I opened my laptop.

The system that had worked perfectly the night before didn't work anymore.

A bug.

I'll fix it in an hour, I told myself.

Three days later, I was still fixing it.

Because every bug you solve somehow creates another one somewhere else.

The hardest part wasn't fixing the bug.

The hardest part was that it never left my head.

Not when I was eating.

Not when I was playing with my five-year-old son.

Not when I was talking to my wife.

Not when I was walking through the forest with Aramis.

Outwardly I was present.

Inside, the conversation never stopped.

Does this still make sense? Should I keep going?

Or should I stop before it's too late?

The worst place isn't your office.

It's your bed.

When everything is quiet.

And your mind starts playing every possible version of the future.

A thousand scenarios. Most of them ending badly.

I'll be honest.

If someone had offered me a stable job that night, I probably would have taken it.

Not because I stopped believing in Atom Foundry.

Because I wanted the noise inside my head to stop.

But something happened the next morning.

Nothing changed. No customer suddenly appeared. No investor called. No breakthrough.

I simply opened the laptop again.

And that is when I realized something.

This isn't a sign that you're building the wrong thing.

It's simply part of building something that has never existed before.

Every founder fights competitors.

The hardest fight is usually against your own thoughts.

Today, when those evenings happen, I don't panic anymore.

I know they will come. I know they will leave. And I know what happens the next morning.

I'll make breakfast. Take my son to kindergarten. Sit down at the same desk. Open the same laptop.

And keep building.

One day, people might only see the finished company.

They will never see the nights when nothing felt certain.

Maybe that's okay.

Because every company has two stories.

The one everyone sees. And the one only the founder remembers.

And yes, Aramis slept through the whole thing.

He never once questioned the plan.

Maybe I should learn something from him.

From time to time, I'll share a Founder's Reality Check. Not startup advice. Not growth hacks. Just honest stories from the journey of building something from nothing.
