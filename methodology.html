Scoring Model

How the score is calculated

The AI Readiness Score is a 0 to 100 composite score across 6 factors. Each factor is weighted by its proven impact on AI agent recommendation behavior. Scores are calculated from a direct automated scan of the store's homepage and product pages. Each missing factor directly reduces your ability to be recommended by AI agents. It is a revenue leak.

A score below 50 means your store is effectively invisible in AI-driven discovery. A score above 90 means AI agents can confidently find, read, and recommend you. There is no middle ground. AI agents either read your store or they skip it.

Factor Definitions

The 6 factors: what we measure and why

01
Structured Data (Schema)
25% weight
Missing schema is the single biggest revenue leak in AI discovery. 72% of stores we scanned have this gap.

JSON-LD structured data is the primary language AI agents use to understand what a store sells, at what price, and how trustworthy it is. Without it, agents must infer product information from raw HTML, a process prone to errors and omissions that leads to the store being skipped in recommendations.

This factor measures the presence and completeness of schema markup including Product, Organization, BreadcrumbList, and AggregateRating. Each missing schema type directly reduces an agent's confidence in recommending the store.

Product schema Organization schema BreadcrumbList AggregateRating JSON-LD format Schema completeness
02
Content Quality & Specificity
20% weight

AI agents match buyer queries against product content. Vague descriptions like "quality products" cannot be matched to a specific buyer query. Specific, benefit-focused content like "easy-care indoor plants delivered fresh to your door" gives agents exact signals to match against buyer intent.

This factor measures product description length, keyword specificity, presence of benefit statements, FAQ content, and image alt text quality. Content that answers "why buy this?" outperforms generic descriptions in AI recommendations.

Description length 150+ words Benefit-focused copy FAQ presence Image alt texts Keyword specificity
03
Trust Signals
20% weight
64% of stores we scanned have missing or unreadable trust signals. This is a direct revenue leak — agents skip stores they cannot verify as trustworthy.

AI agents are being trained to recommend trustworthy stores. Trust signals such as reviews, return policies, and security indicators are machine-readable proof that a store is safe to recommend. Stores with visible, structured trust signals are significantly more likely to appear in AI recommendations.

The key distinction: trust signals must be readable by a crawler, not just visually present on the page.

Reviews count visible Star ratings schema Return policy text SSL certificate Trust badges Shipping information
04
Technical Readability
15% weight

AI agents crawl stores similarly to search engine bots. A technically inaccessible store with slow load times, a missing sitemap, or crawl blocks cannot be fully indexed by AI agents, regardless of content quality. Technical readability is the foundation that all other factors depend on.

This factor measures Core Web Vitals scores via PageSpeed API, presence of sitemap.xml and robots.txt, mobile viewport configuration, and page load speed. A store that loads in 6 or more seconds on mobile loses AI visibility before content is even evaluated.

robots.txt present sitemap.xml present PageSpeed score 70+ Mobile viewport Load time 3s or less Crawlability
05
Price Visibility
12% weight
58% of stores are invisible to AI price comparisons — a direct revenue leak that removes the store from buyer consideration entirely.

When a buyer asks an AI agent to compare products by price, the agent needs machine-readable pricing data. Price hidden behind JavaScript, missing schema markup, or absent currency labels cannot be parsed by AI agents, removing the store from price-based comparisons.

This factor measures whether product pricing is visible in the initial HTML without JavaScript execution, whether it carries proper schema markup including schema:price and schema:priceCurrency, and whether the currency is explicitly labeled on the page.

Price in HTML (no JS needed) schema:price markup schema:priceCurrency Currency label visible Sale price structure
06
Brand Entity Clarity
8% weight

AI agents are trained to recognize and recommend established brands. Brand entity clarity, meaning consistent name, address, and contact information across the web, helps AI agents identify and trust a store as a legitimate, established entity rather than an anonymous merchant.

This factor measures the presence of an About page, social media links, NAP consistency across Name, Address, and Phone, and brand mentions that help AI agents build a confident understanding of who the store is.

About page present Social links in HTML NAP consistency Contact information Brand schema
