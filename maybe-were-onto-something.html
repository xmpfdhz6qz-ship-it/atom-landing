Founder's Reality Check · Entry 006

Maybe We're Onto Something.

Daniel Pokorny · Solo founder, Atom Foundry

For a long time, I kept asking myself the same question.

Does this actually make sense?

Not whether I could build it.

I knew I could build it.

The question was whether anyone else would care.

I had spent months building Atom Foundry.

Researching. Writing. Building systems. Fixing bugs. Publishing research. Sending emails.

Trying to explain something that was still very new.

And sometimes it felt like I was talking to an empty room.

Then something started to change.

Very slowly.

People started replying.

Not customers.

Not people asking for pricing.

Just people who understood what we were trying to do.

They started saying that the research made sense.

That the problem was real.

That what we were looking at was important.

Some people started asking questions. Some started discussing the ideas. Some started using the language we had created.

And then someone wrote about Atom Foundry.

That moment felt different.

Because until then, everything existed inside my own system. My website. My research. My documents. My ideas.

Now someone outside of it was talking about it.

That was the first time I thought

Maybe we are onto something.

It was not success.

It was not proof that Atom Foundry would work.

It was not a customer.

Nothing suddenly changed overnight.

But something inside me changed.

For the first time, I had evidence that this was not only happening inside my head.

Someone else could see it.

Someone else understood it.

Someone else thought it was worth talking about.

And that matters more than I expected.

When you are building something new, you spend a lot of time wondering if you are wrong.

You can build the product. You can publish the research. You can create the website. You can send hundreds of emails.

But none of that tells you if people actually care.

Then one person responds.

Then another.

Then someone shares your work.

Then someone writes about it.

Then someone tells you that your research changed how they think about the problem.

Those moments are small.

But they start to add up.

They give you something you cannot build with code.

Belief.

Not blind belief.

Evidence based belief.

There is still a long way to go.

We still need customers. We still need revenue. We still have a lot to build.

I still have days when I wonder if this will work.

But now I have something I did not have before.

A few signals from the outside world saying

Keep going.

Maybe this is real.

Maybe we are onto something.

And honestly

Sometimes that is all a founder needs to hear.

The story is still being written.

I do not know how it ends.

But for the first time in a while

I am not asking myself if I should keep building.

I am asking myself what we should build next.

From time to time, I will share a Founder's Reality Check. Not startup advice. Not growth hacks. Just honest stories from the journey of building something from nothing.
