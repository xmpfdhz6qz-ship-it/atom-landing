Founder's Reality Check · Entry 009

The Best Part of My Day Has Nothing to Do With Atom Foundry.

Daniel Pokorny · Solo founder, Atom Foundry

I work on Atom Foundry every day.

Morning.

Afternoon.

Evening.

Sometimes late into the night.

There is always something to build.

Something to research.

Something to fix.

Something to understand.

And honestly...

I think about Atom Foundry far more than I probably should.

I think about it while eating.

While walking.

Sometimes even while talking.

But there are moments when it has to wait.

And I have learned that those moments matter.

Every afternoon, I go to pick up my son from kindergarten.

And almost every day, the same thought comes back to me.

This little person is one of the most important things in my entire world.

My wife comes first.

Without her, none of this would even be possible.

She is my biggest supporter.

My trust.

My direction.

But my son is five.

And he will only be five once.

That matters.

When I pick him up, we often go outside together.

If it isn't raining, we go into the forest.

He goes to a forest kindergarten, so the forest is a normal part of his world.

Sometimes we look for animal tracks.

Sometimes we just walk.

Sometimes he tells me something that makes absolutely no sense to me.

And for a while...

I'm not thinking about Atom Foundry.

I'm just there.

That's probably the only time I truly stop thinking about work.

When I'm doing something with my hands.

Walking.

Playing basketball.

Playing with my son.

Doing something physical.

If I'm sitting still...

my brain is usually back at work.

Even when I'm eating.

Which my wife would probably confirm.

A few weeks ago, we went on a trip with some friends.

We walked up to a lookout tower.

Everything was fine.

We talked.

The kids played.

We walked through the forest.

Then we stopped at a small shelter so the kids could have a snack.

Everyone sat down together.

My wife.

Our friends.

The kids.

I sat a few meters away.

Not because I didn't want to be with them.

I just needed a moment.

I looked into the forest.

And for some reason, I started thinking about the future.

My family.

Atom Foundry.

What we're building.

Where all of this could go.

And I had that feeling again.

That quiet feeling that says

You're going in the right direction.

I don't know how to explain it.

I just know it when I feel it.

Of course I feel guilty sometimes.

Every time I take an hour away from work, there's a part of my brain saying

You should be working.

But I also know myself.

If I never step away, eventually I won't want to work at all.

And when I feel that happening, I stop.

Because forcing myself to work when my head isn't there doesn't help Atom Foundry.

It usually makes everything worse.

Sometimes the most productive thing I can do is close the laptop.

My wife asks me what I worked on that day.

I try to explain.

She doesn't know much about AI or IT, so sometimes the explanation gets complicated.

But she listens.

And listens.

And listens.

She lets me talk about AI, research, systems, data and whatever else is currently occupying my brain.

She probably understands about ten percent of it.

But she understands me.

That's enough.

And one day, when Atom Foundry has stable revenue, she wants to work with me.

She knows social media.

She understands people.

And she is already looking forward to becoming part of the company.

I am looking forward to that day too.

I've given Atom Foundry almost everything I have.

My free time.

My weekends.

Time with friends.

Time for myself.

When my wife and son go somewhere to see friends, I sometimes stay home and work.

Not because I don't want to go.

Because right now, I want to build.

I believe that the time will come when I won't have to make that choice anymore.

But there are some things I will never sacrifice.

If my wife needs me...

I'm there.

If my son asks

Dad, will you play with me?

The answer is always yes.

Always.

And then there is Aramis.

He has absolutely no respect for my roadmap.

He doesn't care about deadlines.

He doesn't care about AI Commerce.

And he definitely doesn't care if I'm in the middle of solving the most complicated problem of the day.

He just looks at me.

Sometimes he scratches my leg.

Sometimes he brings me a toy.

Sometimes he just wants to be petted.

And somehow...

he seems to do it most often when my energy is at its worst.

I don't know if he knows.

Maybe he does.

Either way...

I can't look into those eyes and say no.

I believe Atom Foundry will succeed.

I don't know exactly when.

But I believe it will.

And when that happens, I don't think I'll look back and regret the decisions I made.

Because every decision I make is the best decision I can make with what I know at that moment.

That's all anyone can really do.

Build.

Learn.

Adjust.

Keep going.

And maybe one day, when my son is older, someone will ask him what his dad did when he was five.

Maybe he'll remember the late nights.

Maybe he'll remember the laptop.

Maybe he'll remember all the strange things I was always talking about.

But I think he'll probably just say

Dad built robots.

And honestly...

I'll be perfectly happy with that.

From time to time, I will share a Founder's Reality Check. Not startup advice. Not growth hacks. Just honest stories from the journey of building something from nothing.
