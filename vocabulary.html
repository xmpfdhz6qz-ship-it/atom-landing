AI Commerce Score TM
Core Score
The single 0 to 100 number measuring how well AI shopping agents can understand, trust, and recommend a store.
▾
The AI Commerce Score is the composite 0 to 100 measurement of how well AI shopping agents can understand, trust, and recommend a store across 8 scored factors. It is the single most important number in AI Commerce Intelligence. Every store Atom Foundry scans receives one.
45/100
average AI Commerce Score across 6,789 scanned stores

Why it matters
AI shopping agents evaluate hundreds of signals before recommending a store. The AI Commerce Score compresses all of that complexity into one actionable number. A score below 50 means AI systems are actively skipping your store. A score above 85 means AI agents can confidently recommend you.
Example: A beauty brand scores 38 out of 100. The score reveals that AI agents cannot extract product intent, trust signals are missing, and prices render via JavaScript. Fixing these three issues moves the score to 61 and brings the store into AI recommendation flows.

Recommendation Share TM
Key Metric
The percentage of high-intent buying prompts in which a brand appears in AI recommendations.
▾
Recommendation Share is the percentage of high-intent buying prompts relevant to a brand's niche in which that brand appears in AI recommendations. It is the AI-era equivalent of search market share. Where SEO tracked keyword rankings, AI Commerce Intelligence tracks Recommendation Share.
Why it matters
A brand can have a high AI Commerce Score but still have low Recommendation Share if it is not appearing in the actual prompts buyers are asking. Recommendation Share is the real-world test. It answers the most important question: when buyers ask AI to recommend a product in your category, do you show up?
Example: A sports nutrition brand appears in 18 out of 100 high-intent buyer prompts tested in its niche. Its Recommendation Share is 18%. The top competitor in the same niche scores 41%. That 23-point gap is the AI revenue opportunity.

AI Visibility Risk TM
Risk Layer
The score zone below 50. Stores here are systematically excluded from AI shopping recommendations.
▾
AI Visibility Risk is the score zone for stores with an AI Commerce Score below 50. Stores in this zone have critical gaps in the signals AI shopping agents need to understand, trust, and recommend them. They are not ranked lower. They are actively excluded from AI recommendation flows entirely.
Why it matters
52% of the 6,789 stores Atom Foundry has scanned score below 50. That means more than half of all DTC stores carry AI Visibility Risk today. As AI shopping agents drive a growing share of purchase decisions, every day in this zone costs revenue. The gap compounds over time.
Example: A home goods brand scores 43 out of 100. When a buyer asks ChatGPT for the best minimalist furniture brands, this store does not appear. A competitor scoring 71 gets recommended instead. The home goods brand carries AI Visibility Risk.

Machine-Readable Commerce TM
Core Concept
Ecommerce infrastructure structured so AI systems can parse, verify, and act on product and store data without human assistance.
▾
Machine-Readable Commerce is the practice of structuring product data, trust signals, pricing, and commerce information so AI systems can parse and verify it without human assistance. It goes beyond schema markup. It is the complete architectural approach to making a store legible to AI agents.
Why it matters
Most ecommerce stores are built for humans. Beautiful design, emotional copy, visual storytelling. AI agents cannot see any of that. They read structured signals. A store that is machine-readable gets recommended. A store that is not gets skipped, regardless of how good the product is.
Example: A skincare brand has beautiful product photography and compelling copy, but prices render via JavaScript, there is no Product schema, and the return policy is in a modal that AI cannot read. The store is not machine-readable. Its AI Commerce Score reflects that gap.

Recommendation Confidence TM
Score Factor
How strongly AI systems will recommend a store, ranging from a weak mention to a confident top recommendation.
▾
Recommendation Confidence measures how strongly and confidently AI systems recommend a store when it does appear in responses. A store might get mentioned but with low confidence language like "might work for you," or it might get strongly endorsed as a top recommendation. The difference between these two outcomes is Recommendation Confidence.
Why it matters
Appearing in AI responses is not enough. How you appear matters. A weak mention in a list of ten options converts very differently from a strong first recommendation with confident language. Recommendation Confidence determines the quality of your AI visibility, not just the quantity.
Example: AI response with low confidence: "You might also consider Brand X, though I have limited information about their return policy." High confidence: "Brand X is one of the most consistently recommended options in this category, with strong reviews and clear pricing." Same brand, very different recommendation quality.

Semantic Clarity TM
Score Factor
How clearly AI can understand what a store sells, who it serves, and why it is different.
▾
Semantic Clarity measures whether AI systems can clearly understand a store's product category, target customer, unique positioning, and use case. It is scored across hero specificity, USP clarity, description quality, FAQ presence, and the absence of generic or ambiguous copy.
Why it matters
AI agents match stores to buyer queries based on semantic understanding. If AI cannot clearly determine what a store sells and who it is for, it cannot confidently match that store to any buyer query. Generic copy like "premium quality products" tells AI nothing. Specific positioning like "minimalist skincare for sensitive skin" is what AI can match to actual buyer intent.
Example: "We make great products for everyone" scores very low on Semantic Clarity. "Organic dog food formulated for senior dogs with joint issues" scores high. AI can immediately match the second store to buyer queries like "best senior dog food" or "joint health dog food."

AI Extractability TM
Score Factor
How easily AI systems can pull structured answers from a store's content and pages.
▾
AI Extractability measures how easily AI systems can chunk, parse, and extract answers from a store's content. It covers heading hierarchy, FAQ schema, paragraph structure, server-side rendering, JSON-LD coverage, and the overall cleanliness of HTML. High extractability means AI can pull answers effortlessly. Low extractability means AI skips the store even if the content is good.
Why it matters
AI does not read pages the way humans do. It chunks content, extracts structured answers, and evaluates those answers against buyer queries. A store with excellent products but poor extractability is invisible to AI. This is one of the three core dimensions in AI Commerce Intelligence alongside Authority and Relevance.
Example: A wellness brand has a detailed FAQ section but it renders entirely in JavaScript. AI cannot read it. The same content in server-rendered HTML with FAQ schema markup would score highly on AI Extractability and appear in AI responses to buyer questions about that brand.

AI Trust Graph TM
Core Concept
The network of external mentions, citations, reviews, and third-party validation that AI uses to verify and trust a brand.
▾
The AI Trust Graph is the network of external signals AI uses to validate a brand's trustworthiness and authority. These include Reddit mentions, LinkedIn posts, press coverage, review platform presence, citations in articles, directory listings, and any external content that confirms the brand exists and is credible. AI does not just trust what a brand says about itself.
Why it matters
AI systems are trained on the entire internet. They know what people say about brands, not just what brands say about themselves. A store with no AI Trust Graph presence is treated as unverified. A store with strong Reddit presence, press coverage, and review platform citations gets a much higher trust baseline in AI recommendations.
Example: Brand A has a beautiful website but no Reddit mentions, no press, and no review platform presence. Brand B has an average website but 200 Reddit mentions, three press articles, and 500 Trustpilot reviews. AI recommends Brand B. External Authority Signals win.

Prompt Visibility TM
Key Metric
Whether and how often a brand appears when buyers query AI systems with purchase-intent prompts.
▾
Prompt Visibility is whether a brand appears when buyers submit purchase-intent prompts to AI systems. It is the foundational measurement in AI Commerce Intelligence. A brand can have a high AI Commerce Score but zero Prompt Visibility if it has not yet been indexed by AI training data or if its positioning does not match buyer query language.
Why it matters
Prompt Visibility is the direct link between AI Commerce Intelligence and revenue. Every buyer prompt that AI answers without mentioning your brand is a potential sale that went to a competitor. Measuring Prompt Visibility across hundreds of relevant buyer queries reveals the exact scale of the AI visibility gap.
Example: A pet food brand tests 50 buyer prompts including "best dog food for puppies," "organic puppy food," and "vet-recommended puppy food." It appears in 6 of 50. Its Prompt Visibility rate is 12%. The category leader appears in 38 of 50, a Prompt Visibility rate of 76%.

Hidden Authority TM
Risk Layer
A store with real authority and expertise but poor extractability. AI knows the brand but cannot pull answers to recommend it.
▾
Hidden Authority describes a store that has real authority, established expertise, and genuine trust signals but scores poorly on AI Extractability. AI systems may recognize the brand but cannot extract structured answers from the store's content. The result: AI knows about the brand but cannot confidently recommend it when buyers ask specific questions.
Why it matters
This is the most fixable AI visibility problem because the brand has already done the hard work of building authority. The fix is purely technical. Improving heading structure, adding FAQ schema, ensuring server-side rendering, and cleaning HTML architecture can move a Trusted But Invisible brand into AI recommendation flows quickly.
Example: An established wellness brand with 10 years of press coverage, 2,000 Trustpilot reviews, and active Reddit communities still scores 41 on AI Commerce Score because its FAQ is JavaScript-rendered and has no schema markup. Fix the extractability and the authority converts to recommendations.

Unverified Visibility TM
Risk Layer
A well-structured store with good extractability but no external authority. AI can read it but cannot verify it.
▾
Unverified Visibility describes a store with excellent technical structure, good schema markup, and clean extractability but with no external mentions, citations, or third-party validation. AI systems can read and parse the store perfectly but have no external signals to confirm the brand is legitimate and trustworthy.
Why it matters
AI systems do not blindly trust everything they can read. They cross-reference what a store says about itself against what the broader internet says. A store with zero external mentions is treated with uncertainty. Building External Authority Signals through press, Reddit, review platforms, and community mentions converts Visible But Unverified stores into AI-recommended brands.
Example: A new DTC brand launches with a technically perfect Shopify store, complete Product schema, FAQ schema, and clean HTML. But it has no press coverage, no Reddit mentions, and no review platform presence yet. It scores 62 on technical factors but only 18 overall because AI cannot verify it externally.

Intent Misalignment TM
Risk Layer
A brand with authority and good structure whose content does not match how buyers actually query AI systems in that category.
▾
Intent Misalignment describes a store that has real authority and good extractability but whose content does not match the actual language and intent of buyer queries. The store is credible and technically solid, but its positioning, copy, and content are misaligned with how buyers actually ask AI for recommendations in that category.
Why it matters
AI matches stores to buyer queries semantically. If a buyer asks "best collagen for runners" and a supplement brand only talks about general wellness without mentioning athletes, running, or performance recovery, AI will not match it to that query. The fix is content strategy, not technical optimization.
Example: A supplement brand has strong authority and clean structure but focuses all content on general wellness messaging. Buyer queries like "best protein for marathon training," "collagen for joint recovery," and "supplements for endurance athletes" never match the brand. Rewriting content around buyer prompt language fixes this entirely.

Recommendation Velocity TM
Monitoring Metric
The rate of change in a brand's AI Commerce Score and Recommendation Share over time.
▾
Recommendation Velocity measures how quickly a brand's AI Commerce Score and Recommendation Share are changing over time. Positive velocity means the brand is becoming more visible in AI recommendation flows. Negative velocity means competitors are gaining ground. It is the momentum metric for AI Commerce Intelligence.
Why it matters
A static score at any point in time tells you where you are. Recommendation Velocity tells you which direction you are heading and how fast. A brand with a score of 55 but positive velocity of plus 8 per month is on track to reach Moderately Recommendable. A brand at 68 with negative velocity is losing ground to competitors who are improving faster.
Example: In January, a fashion brand scores 48. In March it scores 61. Its Recommendation Velocity is plus 6.5 per month. That velocity, combined with the direction, signals that the brand will reach Moderately Recommendable within two months if the trajectory holds.

Recommendation Graph TM
Core Concept
The relationship network connecting brands, products, trust signals, citations, and semantic relevance across AI systems.
▾
The Recommendation Graph is the network of relationships that AI systems build between brands, products, trust signals, citations, buyer intent, and semantic relevance. It is the underlying structure AI uses to decide which brands belong together, which brands compete, and which brand best answers a given buyer query. Atom Foundry maps this graph across 6,789 scanned stores.
Why it matters
The Recommendation Graph is where AI Commerce Intelligence becomes infrastructure, not just a score. Understanding where your brand sits in the graph, which competitors are adjacent to you, and which trust signals connect you to buyer queries is the foundation of long-term AI visibility strategy. This is the moat. Brands that understand their position in the Recommendation Graph will dominate AI recommendations.
Example: In the skincare niche, AI builds a Recommendation Graph where brands cluster around signals like "sensitive skin," "clean ingredients," "dermatologist-recommended," and "sustainable packaging." A brand optimizing for "clean ingredients" and "sensitive skin" signals enters the graph node most frequently queried by buyers and gets recommended more often.

Recommendation Density TM
Key Metric
How often a brand appears across adjacent buying intents, not just its primary category queries.
▾
Recommendation Density measures how broadly a brand appears across adjacent buyer intents and related query clusters, not just its primary category. A brand with high Recommendation Density appears when buyers ask about its core product, and also when they ask about related problems, comparisons, and adjacent use cases. It is a measure of how deeply a brand is embedded in AI recommendation flows.
Why it matters
Recommendation Share measures presence within a defined prompt set. Recommendation Density measures how far that presence extends across related intent clusters. A brand with high density is more resilient to changes in how buyers phrase queries, more likely to appear in discovery flows, and more deeply embedded in the AI recommendation layer than brands with narrow, category-only visibility.
Example: A running shoe brand appears in 71% of "best running shoes" prompts. High Recommendation Share in its primary category. But it also appears in "best shoes for flat feet," "marathon training gear," "injury prevention footwear," and "lightweight trail shoes." That cross-intent presence is high Recommendation Density.

Recommendation Position TM
Key Metric
Where in an AI response a brand appears: first recommendation, supporting mention, comparison mention, or weak citation.
▾
Recommendation Position tracks where in an AI response a brand appears and with what level of endorsement. The four positions are: first recommendation, the primary endorsed option; supporting mention, included alongside the top pick; comparison mention, referenced when buyer asks to compare options; and weak citation, mentioned briefly without strong endorsement. Position determines conversion quality, not just visibility.
Why it matters
Appearing in an AI response and being the first confident recommendation are very different commercial outcomes. A brand mentioned as "you might also consider" converts at a fraction of the rate of a brand positioned as "the top option in this category." Recommendation Position is the quality dimension of AI visibility. Improving it from comparison mention to first recommendation can double or triple AI-referred revenue without any change in traffic volume.
Example: Brand A appears in 60% of relevant prompts as the first recommendation. Brand B appears in 80% of relevant prompts but always as a supporting mention. Despite lower Recommendation Share, Brand A likely drives more revenue from AI-referred traffic because its Recommendation Position is stronger.

AI Commerce Graph TM
Platform Term
The complete network of relationships between brands, products, trust signals, buyer intents, and AI recommendation systems. The infrastructure layer Atom Foundry maps.
▾
The AI Commerce Graph is the complete network of relationships that AI systems build between brands, products, trust signals, citations, buyer intents, and semantic relevance. It is not a single database or platform. It is the emergent structure across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, and Alexa for Shopping that determines which brands are recommended, in which contexts, to which buyers. Atom Foundry maps each brand's position in this graph as the foundation of AI Commerce Intelligence.
Why it matters
Understanding your position in the AI Commerce Graph is the difference between reacting to AI visibility problems and proactively building a moat. Brands that understand how they are connected to buyer intents, which competitors are adjacent to them in the graph, and which signals strengthen their position will dominate AI recommendations over the long term. This is Palantir-level infrastructure thinking applied to ecommerce.
Example: A skincare brand's position in the AI Commerce Graph connects it to nodes for "sensitive skin," "organic ingredients," "fragrance-free," and "under $60." Each node connects to buyer queries. Strengthening any node connection increases the brand's Recommendation Share for that buyer query cluster. Atom Foundry maps which nodes are strong, which are weak, and which competitors occupy the same nodes.

Recommendation Routing TM
Platform Term
How AI systems decide which brand receives which buyer query. Not whether you are in the graph, but how traffic flows through it to you.
▾
Recommendation Routing describes the dynamic process by which AI systems direct specific buyer queries to specific brands. Two brands can both be present in the AI Commerce Graph but receive completely different query traffic based on their positioning signals, trust scores, and semantic alignment. Recommendation Routing determines not just visibility but the type and intent of the buyers AI sends your way.
Why it matters
Optimizing for Recommendation Routing means going beyond broad visibility and targeting the specific query flows that carry the highest buyer intent for your category. A brand routed to "best eco skincare under $60" queries drives different revenue than a brand routed to "luxury skincare" queries. Understanding how AI routes queries, and actively optimizing for the right routing, is the advanced layer of AI Commerce strategy.
Example: Brand A and Brand B both appear in skincare AI recommendations. But Brand A gets routed to "budget skincare" queries and Brand B gets routed to "premium natural skincare" queries based on their positioning signals. Same category, completely different buyer audiences and average order values. Recommendation Routing explains why.

Persistent Merchant Identity TM
Platform Term
The consistency of how AI systems understand and represent a brand across different platforms and over time. High PMI means every AI system knows the same, accurate story about your brand.
▾
Persistent Merchant Identity measures how consistently and accurately AI systems understand and represent a brand across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Alexa for Shopping, and future AI platforms. A brand with high Persistent Merchant Identity is understood the same way by every AI system: same category, same positioning, same trust signals, same target customer. A brand with low PMI is understood differently or incorrectly by different systems, resulting in inconsistent recommendation outcomes.
Why it matters
Each AI system builds its own understanding of your brand independently. ChatGPT learns from training data. Perplexity learns from real-time crawls. Google AI Mode learns from search signals. If your brand signals are inconsistent across the web, your Persistent Merchant Identity breaks down. You might be recommended well on Perplexity but misunderstood on ChatGPT. Building consistent, structured brand signals everywhere is the foundation of PMI.
Example: A supplement brand positions itself as "performance nutrition for endurance athletes" on its website but has Reddit mentions primarily from casual fitness communities, press coverage focused on weight loss, and a generic llms.txt. ChatGPT understands it as a general wellness brand. Perplexity understands it as a weight loss brand. The store on the website says endurance athletes. Low Persistent Merchant Identity. Inconsistent recommendations across platforms.

AI Trust Layer TM
Platform Term
The complete set of signals AI checks before considering a brand for recommendation. Trust verification happens before any scoring or matching.
▾
The AI Trust Layer is the pre-recommendation verification step that AI systems run before evaluating a brand for any query. It is the stack of external and structural signals that confirm a brand is real, operational, and safe to recommend. The AI Trust Layer includes external mentions (Reddit, press, reviews), schema-verified brand identity, machine-readable commerce signals (return policy, contact info, price accuracy), and absence of deceptive patterns. A brand that fails the AI Trust Layer is excluded from recommendations entirely, regardless of how well it scores on other factors.
Why it matters
The AI Trust Layer is a gate, not a ranking factor. You either pass it or you do not. Brands that pass the AI Trust Layer get evaluated for recommendations. Brands that fail it do not, regardless of how well they score on Semantic Clarity, AI Readability, or any other factor. Building a strong AI Trust Layer is the prerequisite for everything else in AI Commerce Intelligence.
Example: A technically perfect Shopify store with complete schema markup, server-rendered prices, and optimized copy scores 71 on structural factors but has zero external mentions, no press coverage, and no review platform presence. It fails the AI Trust Layer. Its effective recommendation score is near zero because AI will not recommend an unverified brand regardless of structural quality.

Semantic Commerce Layer TM
Platform Term
The infrastructure of intent-aligned product language that bridges what a store sells and how buyers ask AI to find it.
▾
The Semantic Commerce Layer is the structured layer of product copy, FAQ content, schema descriptions, llms.txt positioning, and category language that translates a store's products into the semantic signals AI systems use to match brands to buyer queries. It is the bridge between what a store sells and how buyers ask AI to find it. Without a strong Semantic Commerce Layer, a brand can have perfect technical infrastructure and strong trust signals but still fail to appear in the buyer queries that drive revenue.
Why it matters
AI does not match brands to buyer queries by reading product names or SKUs. It matches by semantic understanding of what a brand does, who it serves, and what problems it solves. The Semantic Commerce Layer is the deliberate construction of that understanding. Brands that build this layer intentionally, using buyer query language rather than brand voice, get matched to more buyer queries and appear more frequently in AI recommendations.
Example: A running gear brand sells "Performance Trail Running Shoes Model XR7." Its Semantic Commerce Layer translates this into: "lightweight trail running shoes for technical terrain, ideal for ultra-marathon training, with aggressive grip for muddy conditions, under $180." That translation is what gets the brand matched to buyer queries like "best trail running shoes for ultras" or "grippy trail shoes for mud."
